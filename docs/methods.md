# Methods

## The rule behind every product

Verify before claim. Every Observatory product carries its source, its
retrieval date, and its method, in the product itself, not in a separate
note. A reader who cannot retrace a claim to a public source and a stated
method is reading a draft, not a product.

## Sources

Public and open sources only. No credentialed, paywalled, or private data.
The source categories products draw on are listed in
[data sources](data-sources.md). A source that cannot be named and linked
cannot be used.

## Collection

Passive-only against third parties. Active tools run on owned or authorized
targets only. Collection records what was taken, when, and from where, so
the same collection can be repeated and compared later.

## Field verification

Public data says what a dataset records. Field verification says what is
actually there. A place condition that matters to a product, a route, a
facility, or a public-use pattern, is checked on the ground before it is
published as fact. Where field verification has not happened, the product
says so plainly instead of implying it.

## Analysis

Every question travels the same cycle:

- Direction: what is being asked, and why.
- Collection: which public sources answer it.
- Processing: how the sources are cleaned, joined, and mapped.
- Analysis: what the processed picture shows, and what it does not show.
- Dissemination: the published product, dated and sourced.
- Feedback: corrections and new evidence from readers and the field.
- Governance: what changes, what is withdrawn, and what is kept.

The software exists to serve the cycle, not the other way around.

## Reporting: aggregates only

Place intelligence is built from public data and field verification,
reported only as aggregates. No individual tracks, no personal data, and no
children's identifiable data, ever. A product that needs individual-level
detail to make its point is the wrong product: aggregate it, generalize it,
or do not publish it.

This rule applies to every product type: map layers, digests, community
briefs, and family guides alike.

## Verification standard

Before a product lands in this repository, it must pass all of these:

1. Every claim traces to a named public source with a retrieval date.
2. The method is written so a second person can repeat it.
3. Place conditions that matter are field-verified, or the gap is stated.
4. All reporting is aggregated; no individual, personal, or children's
   identifiable data appears anywhere in the product.
5. Upstream source licenses are respected, and attribution is given to the
   stated sources.
6. The product is dated, so a reader can tell how old its picture is.

A product that fails any one of these stays a proposal. See
[Contributing](../CONTRIBUTING.md) for how proposals are made.

## Corrections

A published product that turns out wrong is corrected in place, with the
correction dated and the change described. Silent edits to published
products are not made: the record of what was claimed, and when, is part of
the product's honesty.
